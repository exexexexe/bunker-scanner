Bunker Scanner

Finding Cold War and WWII earthworks under Finnish forest canopy, by reading national LiDAR terrain instead of photographs.

Airborne laser pulses slip through gaps in the canopy and hit the ground, so a bare-earth terrain model shows unnatural bumps, pits and dead-straight edges that no aerial photograph can. This runs Finland's open 2 m elevation model through relief visualisations, looks for the beaded chains that anti-tank stone barriers and trench lines leave behind, and checks each candidate against the national heritage register so what is already recorded can be told from what is not.

How to read a candidate

  • Score is a ranking indicator, not a probability. It is a blunt weighted sum, tuned by hand against one known site.
  • "Known" means already-surveyed ground — inside a polygon somebody has recorded — not that the exact feature is catalogued.
  • The aerial frame cannot confirm anything under canopy. It is there to rule candidates out: a track, a ditch, a shed, a quarry.
  • Straight is suspicious, not promising. Finnish farmland is full of machine-cut drainage. Candidates matching that signature are demoted, and shown greyed rather than hidden.

Nothing here is a confirmed find. These are shortlisted terrain anomalies for a human to look at. Anything on the ground sits on someone's land — normal trespass and private-property care applies, and protected sites must not be disturbed.
